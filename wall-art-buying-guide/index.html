How to choose the right wall art

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

The best wall art for 2026 balances your room’s proportions, your personal taste, and the practical realities of hanging, framing, and lighting. Start by measuring your wall and choosing a piece that fills about two-thirds to three-quarters of the available space, then pick a style and material that fits your budget and maintenance level.

How do I choose the right size wall art for my room?

Size is the most common mistake buyers make. A piece that’s too small gets lost on a large wall, while one that’s too large can overwhelm a cozy room. As a rule of thumb, aim for art that spans about two-thirds to three-quarters of the width of your sofa, bed, or the empty wall space you’re filling. For a standard living room wall, that often means a single piece or a gallery grouping that’s at least 24 to 36 inches wide, but always measure your specific space first. Think about viewing distance too. In a hallway or entryway where people pass close, smaller pieces work fine. In a dining room or living room where you view from across the room, go bigger. If you’re hanging above furniture, leave about 6 to 12 inches of breathing room between the top of the furniture and the bottom of the frame, this keeps the art feeling connected to the room rather than floating awkwardly.

Canvas vs. framed prints: which is better for my home?

Canvas prints have a casual, gallery-like feel and often come ready to hang with a wooden frame on the back, so no extra framing is needed. They’re lightweight, less prone to glare, and work well in modern, bohemian, or industrial spaces. The downside is that canvas can be harder to clean, dust tends to settle in the texture, and it may not look as polished in formal settings. Framed prints, on the other hand, offer a more finished, traditional look. The glass or acrylic front protects the artwork from dust, moisture, and fading, and you can choose from a wide range of frame colors and materials to match your decor. The trade-off is that framed pieces are heavier, more fragile, and can reflect light, so you’ll need to consider placement near windows or bright lamps. For a cohesive gallery wall, framed prints often look more intentional, while a single large canvas can make a bold statement on its own.

What are the best materials for wall art that lasts?

For longevity, look for prints on archival paper or canvas with fade-resistant inks. Giclée prints, which use high-quality pigment-based inks, are a popular choice among artists and print shops because they resist fading for decades when kept out of direct sunlight. Canvas prints are typically stretched over a wooden frame, and the quality of that frame matters, a solid, kiln-dried frame prevents warping over time. For framed prints, the glazing is key. Acrylic (plexiglass) is lighter and safer than glass, and it’s less likely to shatter if knocked, but it scratches more easily. Glass offers better clarity and scratch resistance but is heavier and can break. If you’re hanging art in a humid area like a bathroom or near a kitchen, consider a metal or aluminum print, which is waterproof and won’t warp. Wood prints, where the image is printed directly onto a wood panel, have a natural, rustic appeal but can be sensitive to moisture.

How do I hang wall art securely and at the right height?

The standard gallery height is to center the artwork at eye level, which is about 57 to 60 inches from the floor to the middle of the piece. This works well in most rooms, but if you’re hanging above a sofa or console, you can adjust so the bottom of the frame sits 6 to 12 inches above the furniture. For a gallery wall, treat the entire grouping as one large piece and center that overall shape at eye level. Use the right hardware for the weight. For lightweight canvas or small framed prints, a simple picture hook or nail can suffice, but for heavier pieces, use wall anchors or a stud finder to secure the frame to a stud. Always measure the distance between the hanging wire or hooks on the back of the frame, then mark the wall accordingly. If you’re renting, consider removable adhesive strips that won’t damage paint, but check the weight rating, these strips are not suitable for very heavy or large pieces.

Online marketplaces like Etsy and Society6 are great for finding unique, artist-made pieces, and you can often request custom sizes or colors. Etsy is especially good for supporting independent artists, but shipping times and quality can vary, so read reviews and check the seller’s return policy. If you want a truly unique piece, buying directly from a local artist or a gallery is still the best way to get something original, though it may cost more. Always check the return policy and read customer photos to see how the art looks in real rooms.

How do I create a cohesive gallery wall?

A gallery wall is more than just hanging a bunch of frames, it’s about creating a visual story. Start by choosing a theme, such as a color palette, a subject matter (like botanical prints or black-and-white photography), or a mix of frame styles that share a common finish (all black, all wood, or a mix of metals). Lay the pieces out on the floor first to experiment with spacing; a common rule is to keep about 2 to 3 inches between frames, but you can go tighter or looser depending on the look you want. For a balanced arrangement, use a mix of sizes and orientations, a large central piece surrounded by smaller ones works well. If you’re nervous about committing, use paper templates taped to the wall to plan the layout before you hammer any nails. For a more relaxed, modern look, you can lean art on a shelf or mantel instead of hanging it, which allows you to swap pieces easily. Remember, a gallery wall should feel intentional, so avoid clutter, choose pieces that complement each other rather than compete.

Frequently asked questions

Should I buy canvas or framed print for a modern living room?

For a modern living room, a large canvas print often works best because it has a clean, seamless look without a frame that can feel busy. If you prefer a more polished, curated aesthetic, a framed print with a thin black or natural wood frame can also look very contemporary.

How do I measure for wall art above a sofa?

Measure the width of your sofa and choose a piece (or a gallery grouping) that is about two-thirds to three-quarters of that width. Hang the art so the bottom edge is 6 to 12 inches above the sofa back, and center it horizontally.

Is it okay to hang wall art in a bathroom?

Yes, but choose materials that can handle humidity. Metal prints, acrylic prints, or framed prints with a sealed backing are good options. Avoid canvas and paper prints unless they’re coated or framed with a moisture barrier, as they can warp or develop mold.

What’s the best way to hang a heavy framed print without damaging the wall?

Use a stud finder to locate a wall stud and drive a screw directly into it. If you can’t hit a stud, use a heavy-duty wall anchor rated for the weight of the frame. For very heavy pieces, consider using two anchors spaced apart for extra stability.

How can I make a small room look bigger with wall art?

Choose a large, single piece with a light or airy subject, like a landscape or abstract with lots of white space, rather than many small frames. Hang it at eye level and avoid dark, heavy frames that can visually shrink the space. Mirrors can also help, but that’s a different category.

What is the difference between a giclée print and a regular poster?

A giclée print is made with high-resolution inkjet printers using archival, pigment-based inks on fine-art paper or canvas, which produces richer colors and better longevity. A regular poster is typically mass-printed with dye-based inks on thinner paper, which fades more quickly and looks less vibrant.

In shortThe right wall art should make you feel something every time you walk into the room, so don’t rush the decision. Measure your space, consider the material and hanging logistics, and choose a piece that speaks to your taste, whether that’s a bold canvas, a delicate framed print, or a mix of both. With a little planning, you’ll find art that transforms your walls and lasts for years.
