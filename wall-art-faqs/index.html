Wall art questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

The short answer: choosing wall art comes down to size, placement, and personal taste, not complicated rules. Measure your wall, pick pieces that speak to you, and hang them at eye level for a polished look. This guide answers the most common questions about canvas art and framed prints so you can buy with confidence.

What size wall art should I choose?

Size is the most common stumbling block, and the fix is simple: think about the wall, not the art. A good rule of thumb is that your art should cover about two-thirds to three-quarters of the available wall space above a piece of furniture, like a sofa or console. For a large empty wall, one big statement piece often works better than several small ones, which can feel lost. If you’re hanging above furniture, leave a gap of roughly six to eight inches between the top of the furniture and the bottom of the frame, this keeps the arrangement feeling connected. For narrow walls, like hallways, a vertical piece or a tall canvas can make the space feel taller. When in doubt, go bigger than you think; most people underestimate how large art needs to be to fill a wall.

Canvas or framed print: which is better?

Canvas prints have a modern, gallery-like feel with no glass, so they’re lighter and easier to hang. They also avoid glare, which makes them a great choice for bright rooms or spaces with lots of natural light. The texture of the canvas adds depth, but it can be harder to clean, dust tends to settle in the weave, so a soft brush or microfiber cloth is your friend. Framed prints, on the other hand, offer a more traditional and polished look, especially with a mat. The glass protects the artwork from dust and UV damage, but it can reflect light, so you’ll want to consider the room’s lighting. Framed pieces are often heavier and require a sturdy hook, but they’re easier to swap out if you like to change your decor seasonally. Ultimately, it comes down to style and practicality, canvas for casual and glare-free, framed for classic and protected.

How high should I hang wall art?

The standard rule is to hang art so the center of the piece sits at eye level, which for most people is about 57 to 60 inches from the floor. This works in most rooms, but adjust for context, above a sofa or bed, the art should sit closer to the furniture, with the bottom edge about six to eight inches above it. In a dining room, you might hang it a bit lower since people are seated. For a gallery wall, treat the entire arrangement as one unit and find the center of that group, then aim for that center at eye level. A simple trick: use painter’s tape to outline the piece on the wall and step back to check the placement before you commit to a nail. If you’re hanging multiple pieces, keep the spacing consistent, usually two to three inches between frames, so the grouping feels cohesive.

How do I choose art that matches my decor?

Start with the room’s color palette and mood. If your space is neutral, you can go bold with a vibrant abstract or a large landscape to add a pop of color. If your room is already busy with patterns, choose art with a simpler composition and muted tones to avoid visual chaos. A good trick is to pull a color from your existing decor, like a throw pillow or rug, and find art that echoes that hue. Don’t be afraid to mix styles; a modern abstract can work well in a traditional room if the frame ties it in. For a cohesive look, consider the art’s theme, botanical prints for a calm, organic feel, or geometric shapes for a contemporary vibe. The most important thing is that the art speaks to you; if you love it, it will likely fit, even if it breaks a rule.

What’s the best way to hang a heavy framed print?

Heavy frames need more than a simple nail. Use a wall anchor or a picture-hanging hook rated for the weight, and make sure you’re drilling into a stud if possible. For plaster or drywall, a toggle bolt or a molly bolt provides extra support. Always use two hooks or a wire system to distribute the weight evenly and keep the frame level. Before hanging, check the frame’s hanging hardware, if it’s a wire, tighten it so it’s taut, and position the hooks so the wire is centered. A good tip is to use a level and a pencil to mark the exact spot, then step back and eyeball it from across the room. If you’re unsure, a professional framer can add reinforced hanging hardware for a modest fee.

How do I care for canvas and framed art?

Canvas art is low-maintenance: dust it gently with a soft, dry cloth or a brush attachment on a vacuum. Avoid using water or cleaning solutions, as they can stain the canvas. Keep canvas out of direct sunlight to prevent fading, and avoid high-humidity areas like bathrooms unless the canvas is coated. Framed prints with glass need a bit more attention. Clean the glass with a microfiber cloth and a glass cleaner, but spray the cleaner on the cloth, not directly on the glass, to avoid moisture seeping into the frame. Check the backing periodically for signs of moisture or dust, and replace the hanging wire if it shows wear. With proper care, both canvas and framed art can last for decades.

Frequently asked questions

Should I buy art that matches my sofa or rug?

It’s better to pick art that complements the room’s palette rather than matching exactly. Pull a secondary color from your rug or pillows to create harmony without being too matchy-matchy.

Can I mix canvas and framed prints in the same room?

Absolutely. Mixing textures and styles adds visual interest, but keep a common thread, like a similar color scheme or frame finish, to make the grouping feel intentional.

What’s the best way to hang a gallery wall?

Lay the pieces on the floor first to plan the arrangement, then transfer the layout to the wall using paper templates. Keep spacing consistent, and aim for the center of the group at eye level.

How do I know if a print is high quality?

Look for giclée prints, which use archival inks and acid-free paper, and check the frame’s materials, solid wood or metal is sturdier than plastic. Read reviews about color accuracy and durability.

Is it okay to hang art in a bathroom or kitchen?

Yes, but choose pieces that can handle humidity and heat. Canvas with a protective coating or framed prints with sealed glass work best, and avoid placing them directly above a stove or shower.

How often should I change my wall art?

There’s no rule, change it when you feel like a refresh. Many people swap seasonal pieces or rotate art from other rooms to keep the space feeling new without buying new pieces.

In shortThe best wall art is the kind that makes you smile every time you walk into the room. Trust your instincts, measure twice, and don’t overthink the rules, once you know the basics, you’ll find pieces that fit your space and your style. Happy hanging!
